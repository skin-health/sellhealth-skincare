The Dermatology of Active Incompatibility

Why improper skincare layering triggers rapid active breakdown and chronic micro-inflammation.

pH Dependent Ionization

Direct low-pH acids like L-Ascorbic Acid (pH 2.8–3.5) and Glycolic Acid (pH 3.2–3.8) require free hydrogen ion saturation to traverse the lipophilic stratum corneum. Applying higher-pH serums immediately neutralizes acidity, inactivating penetration.

Barrier Lipid Depletion

Simultaneous application of dual keratolytics (such as Retinol with AHA or BHA) cleaves corneodesmosomes and dissolves intercellular lamellar sheets, accelerating transepidermal water loss (TEWL) and retinoid dermatitis.

Redox Metal Chelation

Transition metal complexes like Copper Tripeptide-1 (GHK-Cu) undergo spontaneous electron reduction when mixed with direct L-Ascorbic Acid, oxidizing vitamin C into inactive dehydroascorbic acid while liberating reactive free radicals.
