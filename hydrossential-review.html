Why Does Skin Dry Out and Look Older Faster?

Think of your top layer of skin like a brick wall held together by natural oils instead of mortar. When sun exposure, harsh cleansers, or dry indoor air chip away at that mortar, moisture sneaks out fast.

Dermatologists call this transepidermal water loss (TEWL)—which basically means water is evaporating right out of your skin into the air. When skin runs low on water, tiny surface creases look sharper and your face loses that smooth, bouncy feel.

At the same time, UV rays and pollution create unstable molecules called free radicals. Think of them as tiny sparks that slowly chew up collagen (the stretchy protein ropes that keep skin firm), so skincare formulas often pair water-grabbing humectants with plant antioxidants to calm things down.

What's Inside Hydrossential? Auditing the Core Botanicals

Even without exact percentages posted online, the manufacturer highlights five main plant extracts in the Hydrossential serum. Here is what actual dermatology research says each one does on skin:

1. Japanese Witch Hazel

The Pore Tightener (Hamamelis virginiana): Packed with natural plant compounds called tannins that temporarily tighten pores and cut surface shine. The catch is that alcohol-extracted witch hazel can feel drying on sensitive skin.

2. Aloe Vera Extract

The Soothing Gel (Aloe barbadensis): A classic water-rich plant extract that pulls hydration into the outer skin layer and helps quiet down mild redness or tightness.

3. Jojoba Oil

The Moisture Seal (Simmondsia chinensis): Technically a liquid wax ester that looks a lot like your skin's own natural oil. It acts like a light moisture seal so water doesn't evaporate out of your skin without feeling greasy or clogging pores.

4. Gotu Kola

The Bounce Helper (Centella asiatica): Contains active plant molecules called triterpenes (like asiaticoside) studied in labs for nudging skin cells to make fresh collagen and calm stressed skin.

5. Green Tea Extract

The Spark Shield (Camellia sinensis): Loaded with antioxidants called catechins (especially EGCG) that soak up free radicals triggered by sunlight and everyday air pollution.

Supporting Actives & Secondary Extracts: Across vendor materials and product descriptions, Hydrossential also references familiar helpers and extra herbal botanicals—though again without disclosed percentages:

  • Hydration & Vitamins: Hyaluronic Acid (often used as Sodium Hyaluronate, a water magnet that plumps dry surface lines), plus Vitamins C and E for extra antioxidant support.
  • Secondary Herbal & Citrus Extracts: Rosemary leaf, sage leaf, horsetail (rich in plant silica), hops, rose geranium (Pelargonium), Scots pine bud, and lemon peel extract. While these add botanical scent and mild conditioning, fragrant plant and citrus oils can irritate sensitive skin if concentrated too high.

Side Effects, Irritation Risks & How to Patch Test

Just because a serum uses plant extracts does not make it zero-risk. Natural botanicals—especially witch hazel, citrus peel, and aromatic herbs like rosemary or geranium—are common triggers for stinging, dry patches, or contact redness if your skin barrier is already sensitive, eczema-prone, or rosacea-prone.

Before smoothing any new serum over your whole face, run a quick 24-hour patch test:

  1. Put 1 small drop on clean skin inside your wrist or right behind your ear.
  2. Wait a full 24 hours without washing that spot with harsh soap.
  3. If you notice itching, burning, hives, or lingering redness, wash it off and skip full-face use.
